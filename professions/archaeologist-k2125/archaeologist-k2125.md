Archaeologist

The archaeologist is a specialist who studies ancient societies through the discovery, analysis and interpretation of material remains left by civilizations. Excavates sites to uncover ancient artifacts and structures Analyzes found objects to determine their age, origin, and use Documents and preserves findings for future generations Collaborates with historians and other scientists to interpret historical data Participates in writing research reports and publishing findings Engages the public through lectures and exhibits to share the significance of archaeological finds

### Activities and Skills

- Lead, coordinate a team
- Coordinate the intervention of multidisciplinary teams
- Providing cultural mediation in historic sites
- Advising researchers, institutions, companies on scientific questions
- Advise and provide technical support to students, teachers in their orientation, internship search, project
- Provide courses developing technological, scientific and technical culture
- Chair or participate in an examination board
- Present and explain scientific advances and research work
- Write a scientific publication
- Advising authors on historical contexts for works
- Analyze burials, shards of amphorae, shards of metal, prints, pollen, etc. in the laboratory
- Collaborate with museums, filmmakers, textbook authors, and journalists to ensure historical accuracy in their work
- Collaborate with international teams for research projects
- Collect samples for analysis
- Design and coordinate a program, a research project
- Conduct research on the history of certain works
- Direct excavations at archaeological sites
- Direct research work (thesis, study dissertation, etc.)
- Draw up the plan of the archaeological site
- Discover vestiges, which become objects of science, to reconstruct the way in which the territory was occupied, the activities of men, their lifestyles and social behavior
- Determine the technical constraints of the work linked to the characteristics of the materials, exposure conditions, safety standards
- Establish a study or research report
- Study the feasibility of a project
- Examine and interpret information collected to understand the contexts, causes, and consequences of past events
- Guide excavation equipment (mechanical excavator, for example)
- Involve students in applied research projects
- Conduct post-excavation studies (washing objects, sorting, analysis of each element found)
- Follow a scientific protocol
- Sort, analyze, criticize and compare your different sources to formulate a hypothesis
- Understand, interpret data and technical documents
- Determine and develop methods of research, collection and analysis of data
- Use modeling and simulation software
- Use tools for washing, conditioning and analyzing the excavated soil: sieves, water lances, washing tanks
- Handle transport and lifting tools: wheelbarrow, hand truck, trolley, transport wheels, crowbar, lever, hoist, etc.
- Use equipment for recording discoveries: camera, graph paper, drawing materials (paper, pencils, erasers)
- Use location and survey equipment: survey grid, stakes, rope or cord, GPS
- Use metrology devices and measuring instruments: tape measure, graduated ruler, laser rangefinder, thickness gauges, palmer
- Secure the intervention perimeter
- Manage research budgets
- Evaluate sites to determine their archaeological potential
- Arguing about the importance of site conservation
- Negotiate partnerships with research institutions
- Search for financing, investors
- Lead educational workshops on history
- Provide scientific support to researchers, institutions, companies
- Collaborate with multidisciplinary teams
- Communicate orally during conferences on archaeological discoveries
- Using English in a professional context
- Document archaeological finds for detailed records
- Manage and process data (research, collection, analysis, prioritization, dissemination, classification, monitoring)
- Respect safety rules on excavation sites
- Regularly update your knowledge
- Building a professional network in research
