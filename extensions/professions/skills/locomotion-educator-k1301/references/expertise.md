# Locomotion Educator

## Scope

The locomotion educator plays a crucial role in the rehabilitation and independence of people with reduced mobility. Evaluates patients' locomotion abilities Designs personalized rehabilitation programs Supports patients in carrying out locomotion exercises Adapts sessions according to patients' progress Informs and trains families on technical locomotion aids Monitors progress and adjusts rehabilitation objectives

## Activities and Skills

- Collaborate with multidisciplinary teams for patient monitoring
- Encourage the social participation of isolated people
- Support patients in everyday activities
- Advise the person on technical aids (visual aids, walker) or install the equipment (installation of protective barrier, table height adjustment)
- Discuss with the person their feelings (discomfort, satisfaction, etc.) and refer them to the professional concerned (doctor, social service assistant, etc.)
- Training visually impaired and blind people
- Supporting people in everyday activities
- Adapt your practices to support children with disabilities
- Follow up with families
- Design and carry out rehabilitation care
- Contribute to a person's physical well-being
- Dispense or administer medications
- Providing paramedical care
- Identify changes in a person's health
- Conduct an interview, an interview, an audition
- Analyze the life context of a person or family
- Regularly assess the needs of the person or family
- Propose and implement technical solutions (tactile markers, sound alerts, etc.) for visually impaired or blind people
- Handle medical equipment
- Complete medical-administrative documents
- Present the help or awareness activities to the person and define the implementation and support methods
- Set up and lead discussion groups
- Prepare and lead a meeting, a working group, a workshop
- Adapt activities to the physical and mental abilities of individuals
- Respect the rules of Quality, Hygiene, Safety, Health and Environment (QHSSE)
