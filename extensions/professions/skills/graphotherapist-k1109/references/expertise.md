# Graphotherapist

## Scope

The graphotherapist helps individuals improve their handwriting and associated problems. Assesses writing disorders in children and adults Offers personalized exercises to improve the quality of writing Monitors the development of individuals and adjusts sessions according to progress Organizes group workshops for more dynamic interventions

## Related Job Titles



## Activities and Skills

- Advise suitable tools for writing
- Develop an educational project
- Encourage learner autonomy
- Teaching, developing skills
- Propose work and practical exercises
- Adapt educational methods and supports to the age and profile of the patient
- Analyze writing difficulties (slowness, illegibility, pain, fatigue, tension)
- Design and carry out rehabilitation care
- Develop handwriting fluency, speed and legibility
- Develop a graphomotor assessment based on observations and adapted tests
- Evaluate the quality of the graphic gesture, writing and posture of the person
- Observe motor, perceptual and attentional development linked to the act of writing
- Re-educate graphic gestures through motor, sensory and rhythmic exercises
- Update the patient's medical record
- Regularly assess the needs of the person or family
- Update a file, a database
- Use techniques to reduce tension when writing
- Manage calendars and schedule appointments
- Write a report, an activity report
- Ensure the administrative and financial management of an activity
- Collaborate with other professionals (teachers, speech therapists, psychologists, doctors)
- Create a relationship of trust
- Prepare and lead a meeting, a working group, a workshop
- Respect the confidentiality of information
- Regularly update your knowledge

## Professional Approach
