# General education teacher for the sensory impaired

## Scope

The Sensory Impaired Education Teacher is committed to adapted and specialized education for his students. Provides lessons adapted to the specific needs of students with sensory disabilities Uses and masters sign language to facilitate communication and learning Adapts teaching materials to make teaching accessible Collaborates with families and other education professionals to monitor students' progress Participates in continuing education to stay informed of the latest advances in special education Contributes to the social and academic integration of students with sensory disabilities

## Related Job Titles

- Professor of technical education for the blind and visually impaired
- Professor of the national institutes for young deaf people (INJS) and young blind people (INJA) in general education
- Professor of the national institutes for young deaf people (INJS) and young blind people (INJA) in technical education
- Sign language teacher

## Activities and Skills

- Support and develop autonomy in the use of digital tools and digital services
- Supporting students with sensory disabilities in their daily learning
- Adapt teaching materials to the specific needs of students
- Design educational supports and tools (teaching materials, educational software, exam subject)
- Design and implement activities to acquire or maintain autonomy and social life
- Advise learners in the organization of their personal work
- Define a learning objective and the program of educational activities
- Develop an educational project
- Encourage independence in daily activities
- Teaching, developing skills
- Evaluate learning, training or experience acquired
- Evaluate the individual progress of students taking into account their disability
- Train students in the use of electronic reading devices, voice recognition software, amplified audio equipment, etc. to facilitate their learning and autonomy
- Organize knowledge and skills assessments, exams
- Participate in the development of personalized schooling projects (PPS)
- Promote the autonomy of individuals through personal projects
- Propose work and practical exercises
- Preparing students with sensory disabilities for future professional integration
- Use adapted techniques to teach deaf or blind students
- Evaluate the child's autonomy and physical, cognitive and relational abilities in their daily life
- Facilitate the school integration of students with sensory deficiencies
- Intervene with people with disabilities or loss of autonomy
- Adapting braille documents
- Ensuring the transmission of an oral message to deaf or hard of hearing people
- Demonstrate an appetite for using digital software and new technologies
- Accompanying people with reduced mobility when traveling
- Interact with other educators, specialist assistants, speech therapists, occupational therapists and parents to create an optimal educational environment
- Prepare and lead a meeting, a working group, a workshop
- Travel regularly for meetings with health professionals and educational teams
- Raise awareness among all students and school staff of the specific challenges encountered by students with sensory disabilities
- Use suitable tools and materials: electronic reading devices, voice recognition software, amplified audio equipment, etc.
