# Farrier

## Scope

The farrier maintains the feet (hoof, hoof) of equines or cattle in order to guarantee the balance and health of the animal according to its needs and characteristics. Installation of pathological or therapeutic fittings. Can shape iron in full. Advises owners on hoof care practices to prevent disease or injury. Can lead a structure.

## Related Job Titles

- Hoof trimmer
- Cattle pedicure

## Activities and Skills

- Advise, support a person
- Pass on a technique, a know-how
- Adjust the shoes to ensure good balance and support for the horse
- Provide routine care for equines
- Know and evaluate the animals' reception conditions and environment
- Detect traumatic, inflammatory, infectious contraindications and adapt treatment or refer to care partners
- Evaluate an animal's behavior and health
- Identify the reason for consultation, the degree of urgency and inform the animal owner of the treatment arrangements
- Handle animals
- Offer orthopedic treatment
- Carry out personal hygiene, comfort and prevention care
- Make or install a fitting
- Raise awareness of animal welfare
- Select the iron according to the mobility, effort and morphology of the animal and shape it according to the imprint
- Update a file, a database
- Adjusting shaped features
- Apply a treatment, a product
- Master the characteristics of a product or material
- Check the cleanliness of a place
- Disinfect and decontaminate equipment
- Making fittings from metal bars
- Check equipment or material
- Develop prevention actions or rules
- Manage inventory
- Track orders, invoicing
- Organize and plan an activity
- Ensure the administrative management of an activity
- Carry out accounting operations
- Respect the rules of ethics and professional conduct
- Welcome, inform and guide customers
- Manage telephone reception
- Identify the request from the person responsible for the animal and determine the intervention methods (on-site care, transfer, etc.)
- Inform and advise on the type of shoe, care products, the need for veterinary consultation
- Identify intervention priorities (checks, surveys, etc.) based on annual planning and determine appropriate equipment
- Alert, request support or arbitration
- Apply a safety instruction
- Archive files and reference documents
- Collect information about the animal (problems, behavior, history, current treatments, etc.) from its companion or in the medical file
- Use digital tools
